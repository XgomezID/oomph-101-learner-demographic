Welcome! You're joining a community of students who've come to this program from every direction imaginable. Notice that the charts shown here are built using student enrollment data between 2023 and 2026 simply to illustrate, at a basic level, the collective experience that you all bring to the program. Some of you are 22 and just a couple years out of undergrad; others are decades into a career and bring a lifetime of experience with you. Nearly half of you are the first in your family to pursue a graduate degree. One in five identifies as LGBTQ+. You're calling in from more than 15 countries and every corner of the U.S. Wherever you're starting from, you're not the only one who looks like you, thinks like you, or worried about starting this program — you just haven't met your people yet.

Age at enrollment

Take a look around (well, virtually) — your classmates span every stage of adult life. About 42% are under 30, many navigating grad school for the first time. A third are in their thirties, often juggling this program alongside careers and families. And nearly a quarter are 40 or older, some returning to school after decades away. Whatever stage you're at, there's a whole group of people right there with you.

Under 30: 42%, 30-39: 34%, 40+: 23%

Gender identity

Two-thirds of your fellow students are women, and about 18% are men, with a smaller group who identify as nonbinary or genderqueer. Some students didn't share a gender identity at all, and that's okay too — this is a program built to welcome however you show up.

Woman: 67%, Man: 18%, Nonbinary: 2%, Not reported: 13%

Race and ethnicity (IPEDS categories)

Your cohort brings together a real mix of backgrounds — 41% of students identify as White and 17% as Asian, with meaningful representation across Black, multiracial, American Indian, and Pacific Islander communities too. Look closer at self-reported ethnic identity and the picture gets even richer: dozens of students identify as Mexican, Mexican American, or Chicano, and there's strong representation from Filipino, Chinese, and Vietnamese communities as well. Chances are, someone in your cohort shares a piece of your background — and plenty of others don't, which is exactly what makes this community worth building.

White 41%, Not reported 26%, Asian 17%, Two or more 7%, Black 5%, American Indian 2%, Native Hawaiian/Pacific Islander 1%

Geographic distribution

Nearly two-thirds of your classmates are in California, but the rest of the cohort is scattered across Montana, Oregon, Washington, and states all over the country — plus students logging in from more than 15 countries around the world. Being an online program means your closest classmate might be in the next town over, or twelve time zones away. Either way, you're part of the same community.

California 64%, Other US states 21%, International 5%, Montana 4%, Oregon 2%, Washington 2%
